About

I'm studying Electrical & Computer Engineering at the University of Cape Town, which is a fancy way of saying I've spent the last few years alternating between elegant theory in lecture halls and stubborn hardware that refuses to read the textbook. I gravitated toward engineering because I liked the idea of things I could actually point at and say "I made that" — even if what I made occasionally involved smoke.

My coursework and projects have pulled me toward embedded systems, radar, and mechanical design — basically anything where signals, circuits, and physical structure all have to cooperate at once. Along the way I've built things that had to survive outside the comfort of a lab: sensor systems exposed to real environments, control systems that had to behave, and a fair number of prototypes that taught me more by failing than by working on the first try. I've also picked up a habit of automating things that annoy me, which has occasionally spilled over into managing a small operation of my own.

Right now I'm mostly focused on getting better at the things I already like — deepening my grip on embedded systems and radar, and getting more mechanical design reps in — rather than chasing a specific title. If that turns into interesting collaborations, research, or opportunities down the line, great. If not, I'll just keep building things and writing about them here.

Radar-Driven Interactive LED CUBE

This project was developed as part of an experimental art installation combining radar, music, sound design and visual electronics. Starting from the brief of designing the circuitry for a freeform soldered LED display, I developed a 3×3×3 RGB LED cube controlled by an ESP32 and four 74HC595 shift registers. A 24 GHz radar sensor tracks the user's position and communicates with the ESP32 over UART. I developed the software to interpret the radar data, map the user's position within the cube, and control the LEDs through multiplexing. The system was developed into an interactive movement-based game, where the cube generates a target position and the player must physically move to it before the countdown expires. The system incorporates score tracking, visual feedback, animations, and a firework sequence when the player reaches 10 points.

  • embedded programming
  • digital electronics
  • radar-based position tracking
  • multiplexed LED control
  • interactive visual design

CORRAL — Benthic Chamber Enclosure Redesign & Buoy Housing System

As part of a four-person engineering team at UCT, I redesigned the enclosure subsystem for CORRAL — an autonomous dissolved-oxygen monitoring platform deployed in the Langebaan Lagoon intertidal zone. The original chamber required tools and several minutes to deploy, impractical within a narrow tidal window, so I replaced the bolted lid with a tool-free bayonet closure and dual O-ring seal, validated by hand calculations and a Fusion 360 FEA that caught a stiffness failure the hand calcs missed. The prototype was FDM printed and field-tested: the radial seal passed, the face seal leaked, and I documented the root causes with corrective actions for the next iteration.

  • FEA
  • CAD
  • FDM 3D Printing

TheShiftRosterBot: A Multi-Tenant Scheduling Platform for Delivery Operations

I built and operate a production Telegram-based scheduling system for a delivery operation I manage in Cape Town, coordinating shifts for 30+ drivers across two independently-configured branches. The bot (Python, SQLite, running on a Raspberry Pi) handles shift submission, live coverage tracking, first-come-first-served claiming, cover requests, and admin approvals — replacing manual scheduling with no audit trail. When I took on a second branch with entirely different scheduling rules, I redesigned the system into a multi-tenant architecture: one application serving multiple branches, each with its own database and ruleset, sharing a common engine. As it matured I added tested migrations, a shared test suite, and proper deployment discipline after diagnosing incidents caused by uncertainty about what code was live.

  • Python
  • SQLite
  • Telegram Bot API
  • Raspberry Pi

Quantifying Bioturbation-Driven Oxygen Flux in Permeable Sediments: Hydrodynamic Characterization & Field Validation of a Novel Benthic Chamber

Building on my earlier redesign of the CORRAL benthic chamber, I'm now investigating its hydrodynamic performance — specifically its ability to distinguish diffusive from advective oxygen flux in permeable intertidal sediments. Standard benthic chambers are validated for muddy, diffusion-dominated sediments and fail in permeable sands, where they destroy the pressure gradients that drive porewater flow through the seabed. I'm characterizing the chamber's hydrodynamics — pressure gradients under different stirrer configurations, boundary-layer thickness, and mixing efficiency — then using it in two modes (diffusion-only vs. advection-enabled) to isolate the advective flux component. Field deployments in Langebaan Lagoon will compare oxygen fluxes across defaunated sediment, surface topography alone, live sand prawns, and relict burrows, to resolve how much of benthic oxygen exchange sand prawns drive as ecosystem engineers.

  • Hydrodynamics
  • Benthic Chamber Design
  • Field Instrumentation
